What I compared

This is a current buying guide for two large desktop printers, not a deals roundup and not a hands-on test. I have not run these two machines side by side for this article.

  • SV08 is Sovol’s 350 mm, largely pre-assembled CoreXY. Official setup is about an hour at normal pace, and 90% pre-assembled. The official US product page returned HTTP 200. A glass open enclosure is listed on that same page at $179.00.
  • K2 Plus is Creality’s large enclosed 350 × 350 × 350 mm machine. The official store page lists an actively heated chamber, a die-cast enclosed “Matrix” frame, a step-servo motion system, dual AI cameras, and a 4-bay CFS path for up to 16 colors. I did not find the word “CoreXY” on the official K2 Plus pages I opened today, so I am not adding it as a K2 Plus spec. The official motion language is step-servo / FOC, with “XYZE” motors in the official specification block.

When the SV08 is the better buy

If you mainly print PLA, PETG, and similar everyday materials, and you want official 350 mm volume without paying for an enclosed, heated-chamber machine, the SV08 is the one I would buy. The official US price I saw was $529.00 (was $599.00), which is $325.00 below the official K2 Plus Without CFS price of $854.00.

The official SV08 specification table lists the 350 × 350 × 345 mm³ volume, 700 mm/s maximum, 40,000 mm/s² acceleration, and flow ≤30 mm³/s, plus linear rails, four independent Z motors, a dual-gear direct-drive extruder, nozzle temperature ≤300°C, a hot bed ≤100°C, and compatibility with PLA, PETG, TPU, ABS, and more. Official setup is about an hour, 90% pre-assembled. Those maxima are official page figures, not speeds I timed.

The SV08 is open-frame unless you add a kit, and the official page I opened does not sell a CFS-style color unit with the printer. That product page listed the SV08 Glass Open Enclosure at $179.00, with the same $179.00 as the regular price and no separate sale markdown. If you want a more enclosed SV08, budget that kit on top of $529.00. The 5 mm of official height on the K2 Plus is not a reason to spend the extra $325.00.

The Sovol Technology program I use for these links is US-only, and the prices above are official US-store figures. If you are buying outside the US, do not assume the same offer.

When the K2 Plus is the better buy

Pay the extra official $325.00 when you want the enclosed, actively heated chamber and a first-party color system, not merely a 350 mm plate. The official K2 Plus store page describes a die-cast, enclosed “Matrix” frame and an “Actively Heated Chamber for Engineering Materials.” Creality’s official product page lists an active chamber heater, chamber temperature ≤60°C (50°C for PATCH-CF; 60°C for ABS, ASA, and PPS), nozzle temperature ≤350°C, and heatbed temperature ≤120°C. Those chamber figures were not numbered on the store page I priced, so I am attributing them to the official creality.com specification block.

On speed, the store page markets “high-speed” and “high-acceleration” step-servo motion without a number I could copy from that page. The official creality.com specification block lists printing speed ≤600 mm/s, acceleration ≤30,000 mm/s², and a typical printing speed of 300 mm/s (data from Creality Lab). I am not claiming I measured them.

The CFS path is the other clear difference. The official store page says the 4-bay CFS system lets you print up to 16 colors, and it labels the combo as K2 Plus + 1× CFS. The official creality.com page is explicit: the combo includes only one CFS unit, and 16 colors require three additional units, up to four in total. If you want color on day one, use the $1,099.00 (Save $519.00) one-CFS option on that page. The two-CFS Advanced Combo was $1,497.00 (Save $342.40), and the four-CFS High-End Combo was $2,019.80 (Save $330.00). I did not invent compare-at prices for those combos, because the store showed a “Save” amount rather than a struck regular price.

If you only need a larger single-color printer and you do not need the chamber or CFS, the SV08’s official US price is the stronger first spend. I am not ranking print quality, noise, or reliability from a test I ran for this article.

FAQ

Which printer should I buy if I mostly print PLA and PETG?

The SV08 at $529.00, unless you already want an enclosed, heated chamber or official CFS color. The official SV08 spec table already lists PLA and PETG.

When is the K2 Plus worth $854?

When the official enclosed, actively heated chamber and the CFS path are the reason you are shopping. Confirm the live chamber specification at checkout, and decide whether you need the $1,099.00 one-CFS option on day one.

Does the K2 Plus include CFS at $854?

No. The $854.00 offer on the official store page is K2 Plus (Without CFS). One CFS is the $1,099.00 option on that same page, or the $269.00 (was $350.90) standalone CFS accessory listed there.

Is the SV08 enclosure required?

Not for every job. Budget $179.00 if you want the official glass open enclosure listed on the SV08 page. I am not treating that kit as a heated-chamber equivalent to the K2 Plus official active chamber heater.

Will these prices still be here next week?

I cannot promise that. These are official US figures from the afternoon of 21 November 2026. The Sovol links are for the official US store, and that program is US-only. Check the live cart for variant, tax, shipping, and stock before you buy.
