Official causes Creality lists

Creality’s K1C feed-and-clog article describes two main symptoms: the extruder gears idle and no filament leaves the nozzle, or the gears make a clanging noise and still no filament leaves the nozzle. The official causes are:

  • Filament tangled at the spool or at the PTFE-tube inlet.
  • A blockage at the front shell of the extruder kit.
  • A blockage in the hotend PTFE tube.
  • A clog in the throat tube of the hotend.

Work through those in order. A full hotend teardown is not the first official step.

Fixes, in Creality’s useful order

1. Check the spool and the inlet first. Look behind the printer for a tangle at the spool or where filament enters the PTFE tube. If it is wrapped, untangle it and try again before you open the toolhead. This is Creality’s first official check, and it is the one that costs nothing.

2. Heat the nozzle, release the motor, and try to pull the filament. Creality says to turn the motor off on the control panel, remove the blue locking clip, gently pull the PTFE tube from the top of the extruder, set the nozzle to the recommended print temperature, and then pull the filament out firmly once the temperature has stabilized. For jam-clearing heat, the same official article later specifies PLA at 200°C and ABS at 240°C.

3. Read the end of the filament you just pulled. If the tip has a mushroom- or umbrella-shaped flare, Creality says to replace the PTFE tube. That flare is a sign the filament softened and mushroomed in the tube, which will jam the next load even if you push it back in. The official K1C page also listed a 2 m Teflon tube kit at $8.33 (was $9.26) in the accessories list; confirm that live accessory cart if you need the tube.

4. If the filament pulled out cleanly, try feeding it again. Creality’s path is: insert filament into the hotend, close the extruder handle, run Auto Feed, and watch both the nozzle and the gears. If Auto Feed extrudes normally, the immediate problem is solved.

5. If the gears do not rotate during Auto Feed, stop and check the extruder motor path. Official next steps are to inspect the extruder motor wiring and, if needed, treat it as a motor or connection-board issue. That is not a slicer setting, and I am not inventing a voltage or replacement test beyond what Creality publishes.

6. If the gears turn but extrusion is uneven, look at the nozzle. Creality splits this case clearly. If the gears sound normal and extrusion at the nozzle is still uneven, replace the nozzle. If the gears themselves are noisy or damaged, clean or service the extruder assembly, and replace the K1C extrusion kit if the gear is deformed. The official in-stock part I could put in the cart today was the Unicorn Quick-Swap Nozzle Kit. Creality lists it as compatible with the K1C, and as an all-metal, copper-alloy and hardened-steel nozzle in 0.4, 0.6, and 0.8 mm sizes, with an official maximum flow of 30 mm³/s. Let the nozzle cool before you touch it.

7. If you cannot insert filament into the hotend, use the official probe step. Creality says to pull the filament back out and, with the included probe, push remaining debris toward the hotend. If the debris exits through the nozzle, retract the probe quickly so molten plastic does not stick in the throat. If the probe will not pass, treat it as a hotend clog and follow Creality’s clog-removal procedure, or the official wiki service videos, rather than forcing the gear.

8. Use Creality’s official prevention limits after you clear the jam. The official article says to re-level the build platform, not to turn off the cabinet fan, to use official cutter settings, to keep the bed from running too hot (do not exceed 50°C for PLA, or 70°C for PETG), and to make sure the nozzle temperature is not too low. Those bed limits are official prevention figures from that article, not a full material profile.

9. Do the deeper extruder or hotend disassembly only with Creality’s own steps in front of you. The official article and the Creality Wiki include screw-by-screw procedures and videos for a front-cover jam, a hotend clog, nozzle replacement, and extrusion-kit replacement. I am not rewriting those teardowns here. Follow the official steps, power down or set the nozzle to 0°C when Creality says to let the hotend cool, and do not pull on the motor wiring.

10. If official steps still fail, contact Creality rather than stacking slicer changes. Under-extrusion that remains after a clear nozzle, a fresh PTFE tube, and correct official temperatures is not something I will “fix” with an invented retraction or flow percentage. Use Creality’s support path, and keep notes of the temperatures and parts you already changed.

When should I replace the nozzle instead of the PTFE tube?

Creality ties the PTFE tube to a mushroom- or umbrella-shaped filament tip after you pull it out. It ties nozzle replacement to uneven extrusion when the extruder gears are otherwise turning normally.

What temperature should I use to clear a jam?

Creality’s official clearing figures are PLA at 200°C and ABS at 240°C. For everyday printing, stay with the official profile for that filament, and do not run the bed above the official prevention limits of 50°C for PLA or 70°C for PETG.
