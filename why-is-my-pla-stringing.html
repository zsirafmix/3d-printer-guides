Why PLA strings

Stringing is leftover plastic that the nozzle still oozes while it travels through open air. PLA is usually easy to print, but it still strings when the filament is damp, the nozzle is hotter than it needs to be, retraction is off or untested, or the print is running faster than the material’s official window.

eSUN markets PLA+ as an enhanced PLA with higher strength and toughness, low shrinkage, and a ±0.03 mm tolerance on 1.75 mm filament. The official store page also says the formula is intended to reduce bubbles and stringing. That is manufacturer copy, not a promise that a wet or overheated spool will print clean. This is a troubleshooting guide based on official eSUN parameters and standard slicer practice, not a bench test I ran for this article.

Nine fixes, in a useful order

1. Dry the spool first. Moisture is the most common reason “this used to print clean” suddenly strings. Follow the official PLA+ drying line: 50°C for 8–12 hours. Reprint a small torture model, such as two towers with a gap between them, before you change anything else.

2. Stay inside 210–230°C, then try the lower end. If you are already at 230°C and the print is hairy, drop toward 210–215°C and stay in the official window. A hotter melt oozes more during travel. Do not go below 210°C and then blame the filament; that is outside the official range I copied.

3. Set the bed to 45–60°C. eSUN requires a heated bed. A cold bed is not the usual cause of mid-air strings, but it can ruin first-layer adhesion while you are chasing stringing.

4. Use the official 100% fan. PLA+ is meant to cool quickly once it leaves the nozzle. If part cooling is low, the plastic stays soft and can drag into strings. Restore 100% fan, then reprint the same test.

5. Slow down if you are at or above the official cap. The recommended high-speed parameter is under 300 mm/s. If the profile is running faster than that, or you are relying on the “up to 350 mm/s” marketing line, bring print speed back under 300 mm/s. I am not publishing a made-up “best” speed below that official cap.

6. Turn retraction on, then tune it in your slicer. Many stringing complaints are simply retraction left off, set too short, or copied from a different filament. Enable retraction and use your printer’s or slicer’s starting profile for PLA, then adjust in small steps. Because eSUN does not list a retraction value on the PLA+ page, I will not invent a millimeter figure and call it official.

7. Reduce pointless travel through open air. In the slicer, enable combing or “avoid crossing perimeters,” and consider a wipe on retract, so the nozzle travels inside the print or wipes before it jumps. Faster travel moves can also shorten the time the nozzle spends oozing in mid-air. These are slicer tools, not eSUN-published numbers.

8. Clean or replace a dirty nozzle. A partial clog, leftover residue, or a worn nozzle can keep oozing after you have already dried the spool and lowered temperature. If a long print starts clean and then gets stringy, inspect the nozzle and clean it before you keep stacking slicer changes.

9. Store the filament dry after the print. A freshly dried spool can pick up moisture again on the shelf. Keep it sealed with desiccant, or use a filament dry box during and after printing. eSUN sells an official eBOX Lite dryer and storage box on the official store. FAQ

What temperature should I use for eSUN PLA+ if it is stringing?

Stay inside 210–230°C. I would start a stringing test toward the lower end of that official window, then raise temperature only if walls look under-extruded or weak.

Do I have to dry PLA+ every time?

Not every fresh, sealed spool needs an 8–12 hour dry on day one. Dry it when the spool has sat unused and the print starts to string, pop, or look cloudy. The official PLA+ figure is 50°C for 8–12 hours.

Why won’t you give me a retraction setting?

Because eSUN does not publish one on the official PLA+ page I opened. A made-up millimeter figure would be guesswork, and it would be wrong for many printers. Use your slicer’s PLA starting retraction, then tune it after drying and temperature are already in range.

Is the eBOX Lite required?

No. It is an official eSUN dryer and storage box, and it is optional. I could not confirm a clean cart price or stock on the product page today, so check the live listing if you want one. The drying setting to follow for PLA+ is still 50°C for 8–12 hours.
