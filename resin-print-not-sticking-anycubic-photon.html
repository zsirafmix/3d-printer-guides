What “not sticking” usually looks like

Anycubic’s official Photon wiki describes three common versions of this problem: no cured model on the build plate (it may still be in the vat, or it may not have formed at all); only the bottom of the model adheres, while the rest does not continue; or the model detaches during the print. Before you change settings, Anycubic says to clean the resin vat and make sure it contains no cured fragments or other debris. Those chips can sit on the release film, ruin the next exposure, and look like an adhesion problem.

This guide follows that official order, plus Anycubic’s published Standard Resin starting values. It applies to current Photon Mono printers, including the Photon Mono 4 Ultra, which I priced today at $299.00, with the same $299.00 regular price and an estimated ship time of 24 hours. Wear gloves and goggles, and work in a ventilated area, as Anycubic’s resin precautions require.

Official starting settings for Anycubic Standard Resin

The official Standard Resin page lists a general window of 20–40 seconds for first-layer / bottom exposure and 2–3.6 seconds for normal exposure, a print environment of 20–40°C, and a recommended printing environment of 20–35°C. Shake the bottle well before you pour. At 0.05 mm layer height, the same official table gives these starting values:

  • Photon Mono 4 Ultra: normal exposure 2.6 s, light-off 1 s, bottom exposure 30 s, 5 bottom layers, Z lift 5 mm, lift and retract 8 mm/s.
  • Photon Mono 4: normal exposure 2.8 s, light-off 1 s, bottom exposure 30 s, 5 bottom layers, Z lift 6 mm, lift 4 mm/s, retract 6 mm/s.
  • Photon Mono M7: normal exposure 2.2 s, light-off 0.5 s, bottom exposure 25 s, 4 bottom layers, Z lift 8 mm, lift and retract 6 mm/s.

If your Photon is not in that short list, use the official table on the Standard Resin page for that model. Do not start a failing print at 80 seconds bottom exposure if you have not yet tried the official starting value.

Ten fixes, in a useful order

1. Clean the vat and the build plate first. Remove cured chips, wipe the plate, and check that the vat bottom is clear. Anycubic puts this before parameter changes for a reason: debris on the release film will keep the next model from forming cleanly.

2. Confirm the resin is mixed and warm enough. Official Standard Resin instructions say to shake the bottle well and to print in a recommended 20–35°C environment, because low temperature lowers the success rate. If the bottle has been sitting in a cold room, warm it into that official range before you change exposure.

3. Run the official exposure test. Anycubic’s Mono 4 wiki says to remove the vat and plate, place a sheet of A4 paper on the screen, open Tools, then Exposure Test, and compare the control-screen image with the image on the LCD. Do not look directly at the exposure pattern. If the images do not match, stop and contact the seller or Anycubic support; that is a hardware path, not a settings path.

4. Level the build plate. Official Mono 4 Ultra specifications list 4-point manual leveling. The wiki says to place leveling paper or A4 paper on the left of the screen, use Tools and Move Z-axis, wait for the plate to settle (it may dip, lift slightly, then come down again), and feel the resistance when you pull the paper. Repeat on the right. If one or both sides pull too easily, follow Anycubic’s manual leveling steps for your model.

5. Switch to official resin, or confirm the profile. If you are using a third-party bottle, Anycubic’s wiki says to test with official Anycubic resin. If official resin prints and the other bottle does not, the original parameters were probably wrong. Use the official Standard Resin starting table, or Anycubic’s R_E_R_F file on the USB drive, instead of inventing times.

6. Print the official USB test file. On the Mono 4 series, Anycubic names that file TEST.pm4n. The point is to rule out a model that needed more or better supports. If the official test sticks and your own file does not, fix supports and orientation before you keep raising exposure.

7. If the official test still forms nothing, try an 80-second bottom exposure. That 80-second figure is Anycubic’s official next step when the test file leaves a bare plate, not the everyday starting value. The official Standard Resin table still starts much lower, for example 30 seconds on the Mono 4 Ultra.

8. If only the base sticks, increase normal exposure. Anycubic’s wiki says to raise normal exposure when the raft or bottom holds and the rest of the model does not continue. Change one value at a time and keep notes. An Anycubic store guide also suggests adding about 10 seconds of bottom exposure and five extra bottom layers to your current profile; treat that as a later increment, after you have already started from the official table.

9. Check the release film. Official Standard Resin instructions say to confirm the release film is undamaged and that the vat bottom stays highly transparent. The Mono 4 Ultra official page specifies ACF film. A scratched, cloudy, or loose film can leave the model in the vat even when exposure is otherwise correct. If the film is damaged, replace it with the official film for that printer; do not keep printing over a torn sheet.

10. Run mechanical calibration, then stop if it is still a hardware issue. On the Mono 4 series, Anycubic’s wiki includes a mechanical-calibration path: Settings, System, About, detailed information, then tap the device name ten times to open the function. Remove and reinstall the clean plate as the on-screen steps require. If exposure images still do not match, or calibration and official settings still fail, that is the point to contact support rather than to keep adding seconds.

After the print actually sticks

Once the model is on the plate, official Standard Resin post-processing is: wash in 95% IPA (the page lists a wash of at least 3 minutes, and the instructions also mention about 10 minutes in an Anycubic Wash & Cure machine), dry the model, then secondary-cure for 3–5 minutes. FAQ

Should I jump straight to 80 seconds of bottom exposure?

Not as the first change. Start with the official Standard Resin table for your Photon, then use Anycubic’s 80-second wiki step only if the official test file still forms nothing.

Why did my print stick to the film instead of the plate?

That is the same official problem family: leveling, dirty film, weak bottom exposure, or a damaged release film. Clean and level first, then use the official test file before you keep changing the model.

Do I need a new Photon if adhesion fails?

Usually no. Most of the official path is cleaning, leveling, resin, and exposure. If you are buying a current small Photon, the official Photon Mono 4 Ultra was $299.00 today and listed a 24-hour ship estimate, with a 153.4 × 87 × 165 mm (2.2 L) volume and 4-point manual leveling.
